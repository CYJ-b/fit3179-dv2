Coastal Tourism - The Bounce Back From COVID-19
and Its Current State

As COVID-19 came into the picture, tourism in Australia, including coastal, dropped as a whole. Later, as the borders open up, tourism regions across Australia saw a recovery.

However, how much has coastal tourism recovered compared to inland tourism? And how much does it contribute to Australia's economy?

Disclaimer: Tourism regions are determined by Tourism Research Australia. Whether they are coastal or inland was determined based on the proportion of their borders which touches the sea. (taking the region's area into consideration as well)

1. The period the world stopped travelling

As the COVID-19 pandemic hit in 2020-2021, the number of visitors across the globe reduced significantly, with Australia's closed borders playing a major role in the reduction during this period.

The Australian border closure started on 20 March 2020 as a response to the rising number of cases and deaths across the world. It was not until 21 February 2022 that the borders reopened for fully vaccinated individual, following a decreasing number of worldwide deaths.

After the recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic, it can be seen that China and New Zealand reclaimed their top 2 spots while Malaysia dropped to rank 11. This indicates that Malaysian's visiting patterns to Australia may not have fully recovered or that it has been altered.

2. Where visitors go

Almost every international visit lands on the coastal regions, with Perth, Melbourne, Sydney, Brisbane, and Gold Coast being the most popular regions. This seems to hold even across the years.

When comparing 2025 to 2019 for international visits, the coast has recovered greater than the inland. This indicates that coastal regions play a more significant role for Australia's tourism industry.

3. When and why they come

The number of visits for the top 20 busiest coastal seems to follow seasonal trends. Where most visits seem to happen during the summer, which is fitting for coastal regions where there are beaches.

During the closed borders, visiting friends and relatives briefly became the biggest reason for visits to Australia, overtaking holiday as a reason. Furthermore, "visiting friends and relatives" also had the quickest recovery after the opening of the borders.

Comparing 2025 to 2019, international visitors seem to prefer longer stays compared to having short 1-7 night stays, especially "31 or more nights" which seems to have the highest increase in proportion for both coastal and inland tourism regions.

4. Where the businesses are

In 2025, the coast is 35% of the land but holds 88% of tourism businesses, 14 times denser than the inland regions. Big cities such as Melbourne has 21 businesses per km² and Sydney 11, while central regions such as Lasseter have fewer than one per 10,000 km². This highlights the importance of coastal regions compared to inland regions

Number of coastal businesses grew by 14,416 (+4.8%) between 2021 and 2025, where sole traders seemingly having the biggest boom, indicating a shift in tourism businesses industry.

5. What the coast is worth

The coast holds 35% of the land but 88% of businesses, 93% of international visits, 87% of spend and value added (GVA, the value tourism adds to the local economy), and 86% of jobs.

Furthermore, the coastal regions metrics fell further than inland regions in 2020-21 but ultimately climbed higher by 2024-25 for all metrics, especially tourism spendings and the amount of jobs. In addition, the coastal regions metrics not only bounced back, but rose to higher than before the pandemic (2018-2019)

Coastal tourists spent $182.7 billion in 2024-25. Getting around ($54.6bn) and food and drink ($49.1bn) taking up the majority of this value. The significant proportion towards getting around indicates that coastal tourists do not just stay in one place, but rather like to move around. Additionally, this huge amount shows the economic and impact of coastal tourism to Australia.

6. Conclusion

  • Tourism is a mainly coastal business: 87-93% of visits, spend and jobs.
  • Coastal tourism seems to be recovering well from the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic,
    even supassing the values before the pandemic for some metrics
  • Coastal tourism contributes a significant amount of economic value to Australia
